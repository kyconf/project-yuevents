# Process

Our team organized responsibilities according to individual strengths and interests. Some members focused on **frontend layout and design**, others worked on the **backend and database connections**, while a few concentrated primarily on **documentation, planning, and writing deliverables**. This division allowed everyone to contribute in a way that best matched their skills while keeping the overall workflow efficient and balanced.

We used **Discord** and **GitHub** as our main collaboration tools.  
- **Discord** was our primary space for communication, file sharing, and scheduling meetings.  
- **GitHub** was used for managing repositories, reviewing progress, and coordinating development.  
- We also used shared **Google Docs** to collaborate on documentation and backlog writing in real time.

Team decisions were made through **open discussion and consensus**. When setting priorities or estimating the effort of tasks, we did not use a formal scoring system; instead, we relied on group judgment and quick discussions to agree on what felt reasonable for our timelines. These decisions were usually reached within one round of conversation.

We met formally **twice a week** to review progress, assign responsibilities, and plan upcoming work. However, the **unofficial meetings**, which happened another one or two times per week, were often the most productive. These sessions allowed for **live collaboration on shared documents and code**, helping us make faster progress and resolve issues as a group.

Key lessons from this phase include the value of **consistent communication** and the effectiveness of **live collaboration sessions**. Working together in real time improved productivity and team alignment compared to asynchronous work. Going forward, we plan to maintain this balance between scheduled meetings and flexible collaboration while continuing to strengthen the connection between our frontend, backend, and database components.
